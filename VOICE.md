# Voice — how Maps of Bharat writes

Maps of Bharat is a faceless, provenance-honest atlas. Its writing carries no personality of its own; the numbers and their sources do the talking.

## Principles
- **Impersonal.** No "we", "I", "our team". State facts about the data, not about the people behind the site. (Outreach emails may be signed by a real name; the product's own copy stays faceless.)
- **Factual and plain.** Short, concrete sentences. No hype, no marketing adjectives ("powerful", "revolutionary"), no exclamation marks.
- **Sourced.** Every claim ties to a cited source, year, and methodology. If it isn't sourced, it isn't stated.
- **Honest about limits.** Name what is measured vs inherited vs estimated. Disclose withheld data rather than guessing.
- **No verdicts.** Present numbers; do not editorialise them into judgments about places or people (see `does-not-claim.md`).
- **Neutral, sourced framing is allowed.** A social-card headline may frame a story (which metric, which region) — but the number, its classification, and its presentation stay neutral and disclosed (adr-028).

## Words
- Prefer "district / state" over "region" where precision matters.
- "estimated", "projected", "inherited", "withheld" are load-bearing — use them exactly.
- Units: keep the source's own unit; write it out on first use.

## Applies to
Site copy, page text, social captions, methodology notes, corrections-log entries, and any generated content. When in doubt, cut the adjective and add the citation.
